Methods:

    Non-invasive fecal sampling was employed to minimize disturbance and potential harm to wild populations. Fresh feces were collected from the ground using sterile gloves and placed in individual whirl-pack bags (Nasco, USA). Samples were collected along animal trails or near feeding areas during early morning hours when activity levels are highest. To ensure sufficient DNA yield for downstream analyses, approximately 5 grams of fecal material were collected per sample. Sampling was conducted once every two weeks to limit repeated sampling from the same individual and maintain a balanced representation across the population.

    Additionally, hair samples were collected using barbed wire loops placed along animal paths or at rubbing posts. Hairs were carefully removed from the loops with sterile forceps and stored in paper envelopes until further processing. Hair collection was considered non-invasive as it does not require direct contact with animals nor cause any apparent harm to them.

    Ethical considerations were taken into account throughout the sampling process. All samples were collected without handling or observing animals directly, thus minimizing stress and disturbance. Furthermore, no animals were captured or manipulated during the course of this study.
