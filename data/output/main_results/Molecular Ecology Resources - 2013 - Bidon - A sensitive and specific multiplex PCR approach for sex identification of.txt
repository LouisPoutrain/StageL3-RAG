Document hypothétique 1:
--------------------------------------------------
In this study, we employed non-invasive genetic sampling methods to collect DNA from wild animal populations. We focused on fecal samples as they are abundant, easily accessible, and contain sufficient DNA for genetic analyses. Fecal samples were collected using clean gloves to minimize contamination. We targeted known animal trails and feeding sites where fresh feces were abundant. Sampling was conducted every other day over a two-week period to ensure adequate representation of individuals within the population while minimizing disturbance.

Live trapping was also employed as a complementary method in cases where fecal sampling proved insufficient or unreliable. Animals were captured using baited leg-hold traps specifically designed for the target species. Upon capture, animals were briefly anesthetized using an approved sedative to minimize stress and facilitate safe handling. Blood samples (approximately 2 mL) were collected from the jugular vein using sterile needles and stored in EDTA tubes at room temperature until processing.

For both sampling methods, strict ethical considerations were taken to minimize disturbance, stress, and injury to the animals. All trapping sites were checked regularly, and captured animals were released as soon as possible after sample collection. Capture and handling procedures were approved by relevant animal ethics committees and followed guidelines for the treatment of animals in research set forth by the American Society of Mammalogists.
--------------------------------------------------

Document hypothétique 2:
--------------------------------------------------
For non-invasive genetic sampling of wild animals, we employed a passive collection method using hair snares. These devices consist of a piece of wire mesh with barbed wire attached, designed to capture loose hairs from the coat of passing animals without requiring direct contact or manipulation. Hair snares were placed along known wildlife trails in the study area and left for a duration of four weeks before being retrieved and replaced. This approach minimizes disturbance to the animals and reduces the risk of injury compared to active capture methods. A total of 10 hair samples were collected from each individual, with care taken to ensure that multiple hairs were obtained to maximize DNA yield. Samples were stored in sterile tubes at -20°C until further processing for molecular analysis.
--------------------------------------------------

Pour cette étude, nous avons collecté des échantillons de sang et de poils chez des cerfs élaphes (_Cervus elaphus_) en utilisant une méthode non-invasive. Nous avons utilisé des pièges à écouvillon en coton imprégné d'huile pour recueillir les échantillons de sang sur les femelles pendant la période de rut. Les pièges ont été placés dans les sites de frottement et de marquage des mâles, où ils étaient remplacés tous les deux jours pendant une durée totale de quatre semaines. Pour les échantillons de poils, nous avons utilisé des adhésifs spéciaux collés sur les clôtures ou les troncs d'arbres dans les zones fréquentées par les cerfs. Les échantillons ont été prélevés tous les 10 jours pour minimiser l'impact environnemental et garantir la sécurité des animaux.

Nous avons pris plusieurs précautions pour minimiser le stress et les perturbations chez les animaux. Ensuite, nous avons utilisé des pièges non-invasifs qui évitaient tout contact direct avec les cerfs, réduisant ainsi le risque de blessures ou de stress.
