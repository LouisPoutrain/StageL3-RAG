The DNA sampling methods employed for the genetic analysis of wild animals often involve non-invasive techniques to minimize disturbance and stress on the targeted species. In this study, we utilized hair snares and barbed wire loops placed along trails and at known bear activity sites to collect grizzly bear hairs passively. These tools allowed us to capture hair samples without direct contact with the animals. The sampling protocol involved checking the snares and loops every 2-4 weeks for a duration of one year, ensuring that each site was visited during both summer and winter months. A total of 137 hair samples were collected from 50 different individuals, with an average of 2.75 samples per bear. To minimize any potential impact on the bears' habitats, we used non-toxic lubricant on the barbed wire loops to facilitate hair collection without causing damage to the animals or their surroundings. Additionally, all sampling sites were monitored for signs of disturbance and no evidence of adverse effects was observed during the study period.

Document hypothétique 3:
--------------------------------------------------
"To collect non-invasive DNA samples from grizzly bears in the southwestern United States, we employed a spatial capture-recapture (SCR) protocol using hair snares. These passive sampling devices consisted of barbless wire loops attached to trees along trails and baited with scent-laden cotton pads to attract bears. Hair snares were checked weekly for a duration of six months during the bears' active season, from April to October. Upon detection of bear visitation, hairs shed within the loop were carefully collected using sterile forceps and placed in individual Whirl-Pak bags. To minimize disturbance to the animals, hair snares were positioned at least 10 meters apart and no more than one snare was set per square kilometer. This non-invasive method allowed for the collection of genetic material without direct contact with or handling of the bears, thereby reducing potential stress and injury."
