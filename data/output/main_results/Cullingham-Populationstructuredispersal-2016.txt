The sampling method employed in this study involved the collection of non-invasive DNA samples from wild animal populations. The target samples were hair snares and scat collected using passive trapping methods. Hair snares were constructed using barbed wire coated with a synthetic wax-based adhesive, while scat was collected using artificial scent stations baited with anal gland lure. Both sampling devices were checked daily to ensure minimal disturbance to the animals and reduce the risk of predation by other wildlife. A total of 50 hair samples and 100 scat samples were collected from each study site over a period of six months, with an average interval of two weeks between collections. To minimize stress and disturbance to the animals, all trapping equipment was checked before dawn and after dusk, and no handling or direct contact with the animals was required for sample collection. The collected hair samples were stored in individual paper envelopes, while scat samples were frozen at -20°C within 48 hours of collection to preserve DNA integrity.

--------------------------------------------------
The DNA sampling methods used in this study involved the collection of non-invasive fecal samples from wild populations of gray wolves (Canis lupus). The animals were not directly contacted or manipulated during the sampling process. Fresh scat was collected using sterile gloves and placed into individually labeled Whirl-Pak bags containing silica beads to preserve DNA integrity. Sampling was conducted along established wolf pack territories at a frequency of approximately once every two weeks, with an average of five samples collected per territory per year. To minimize disturbance and reduce potential stress to the animals, sampling was performed during periods of low human activity and outside of denning seasons. Additionally, all field protocols were approved by relevant local authorities and adhered to strict ethical guidelines to ensure minimal impact on the wolf populations.
