Document hypothétique 1:

In this study, non-invasive DNA sampling was employed to minimize disturbance to wild white-spotted eagle rays. Shed skin samples were collected passively using cotton swabs attached to monofilament fishing lines and secured in baited traps. Traps were set overnight at known aggregation sites and checked the following morning. Once a ray entered the trap, it would shed skin cells on the cotton swab as it moved around. After several hours of trapping, the swab was removed and stored in a 95% ethanol solution. This method allows for minimal contact with the animal, reducing stress and potential injury.

Document hypothétique 3:
--------------------------------------------------
"For non-invasive sampling of wild animals, we employed a passive collection method using hair snares. These devices consist of barbed wire loops attached to trees or other sturdy structures along animal trails. The loops were baited with scent-laden cotton swabs, which were replaced every two weeks to ensure fresh attractant. Hair samples were collected by rubbing the cotton swabs against the barbs, capturing any hair shed by passing animals. To minimize disturbance and stress to the animals, we positioned the snares at least 5 meters apart and checked them weekly to collect accumulated samples. This approach allowed us to obtain sufficient genetic material while preserving animal welfare."
