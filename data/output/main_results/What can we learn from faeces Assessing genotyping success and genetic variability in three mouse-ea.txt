In this study, we employed non-invasive genetic sampling methods to collect DNA from wild mice populations. We used a combination of live trapping and hair snare traps baited with commercial mouse attractant (Pro-Trap Live Mouse Trap Bait). Traps were set up along transects in the study area and checked daily for five consecutive days. Captured animals were minimally handled, with no more than two minutes of contact, to minimize stress and disturbance. Hair samples were collected from live-trapped mice using a sterile cotton swab gently rubbed over the animal's back. For hair snare traps, we used barbed wire coated with a non-toxic, biodegradable adhesive (Tanglefoot) to collect hair samples passively. Traps were deployed for 48 hours and checked every 24 hours to minimize animal distress. All collected samples were stored in individual Whirl-Pak bags at -20°C until further processing.
