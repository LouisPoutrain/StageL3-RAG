For non-invasive genetic sampling, we collected faecal samples from white-handed gibbons (Hylobates lar) in Khao Yai National Park, Thailand. We searched for fresh faeces along trails and near sleeping trees during monthly visits over a period of one year. A total of 145 samples were collected using sterile gloves and plastic bags. To minimize disturbance, we approached sites cautiously, wearing neutral clothing to blend in with the environment. Samples were stored at -20°C within four hours of collection until DNA extraction. This method allowed us to obtain genetic material without direct contact or stressing the animals, aligning with Taberlet's definition of non-invasive sampling.
--------------------------------------------------

Document hypothétique 2:
--------------------------------------------------
Extraction of genetic material from wild animals requires careful consideration of sampling methods to minimize disturbance and potential harm. In our study on white-handed gibbons (Hylobates lar), we employed non-invasive fecal sampling as it is considered a low-impact method according to Taberlet's definition of invasiveness. Fresh fecal samples were collected manually using sterile gloves under the permission of local authorities, ensuring that no contact with the animal was made during the process. The samples were then stored in RNAlater™ solution to preserve genomic DNA integrity. Fecal sampling occurred once a month at each study site for six months, allowing us to collect sufficient genetic material while minimizing disturbance to the animals and their habitats. Ethical considerations included obtaining necessary permits, maintaining a safe distance from the gibbons during observations, and ensuring that no harm was caused to the animals or their environment.
--------------------------------------------------

Document hypothétique 3:
--------------------------------------------------
In this study, non-invasive fecal sampling was employed to collect DNA from wild white-handed gibbons (Hylobates lar). Fecal samples were collected along established trails and sleeping sites using sterile gloves and plastic bags. Samples were collected every other day over a period of five consecutive days to minimize the risk of collecting multiple samples from the same individual while ensuring sufficient genetic material for analysis. To minimize disturbance, sampling was conducted during the early morning hours when gibbons were less active. All necessary permits were obtained, and the study protocol was approved by relevant authorities to ensure adherence to ethical guidelines and minimize potential harm to the animals.
