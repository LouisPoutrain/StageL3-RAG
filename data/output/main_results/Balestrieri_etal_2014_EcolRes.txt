For non-invasive genetic sampling of wildlife, we employed a passive collection method using hair snares. This technique involved setting up wire-mesh traps baited with peanut butter and oats, specifically designed to capture hairs from the animals' coats without causing physical harm or stress. The traps were checked daily for signs of animal contact and any collected hairs were carefully removed using sterile forceps. Document hypothétique 3:
--------------------------------------------------
For genetic analysis of wild animals, non-invasive sampling methods were employed to minimize animal disturbance and stress. Fecal samples were collected as the primary source of DNA. To maximize sample recovery, hair traps baited with attractive food items specific to each target species were deployed in areas of high animal activity. These traps consisted of a metal frame covered with a fine mesh wire, containing a bait station filled with peanut butter and oatmeal mixture for carnivores or fruit-based mixture for herbivores. The traps were checked daily, and any captured animals were immediately released without contact to ensure minimal disturbance. Fecal samples were collected using sterile forceps and placed in individual Whirl-Pak bags containing silica gel desiccant to preserve DNA quality. Samples were collected at regular intervals of 5-7 days over a period of two weeks to ensure sufficient genetic representation within the population. Animal ethics considerations included minimizing capture duration, avoiding baited traps during mating or breeding seasons, and using non-harmful trapping methods to maintain animal welfare.
--------------------------------------------------
