Document hypothétique 1:
--------------------------------------------------
The DNA sampling methods used for wild animal genetic analysis were non-invasive and focused on collecting fecal samples. This approach was chosen to minimize stress and disturbance to the animals while maximizing sample availability. Fecal samples were collected using sterile gloves and stored in individual zip-top plastic bags containing a small amount of silica gel desiccant to preserve DNA integrity. Samples were collected at each animal's den site once every three days over a period of one month, with a total of nine samples collected per animal. This frequency was deemed adequate to capture within-individual variation while minimizing disturbance. To minimize the impact on animals, all collections took place outside of peak activity hours and no handling or manipulation of the animals occurred during sampling. Additionally, precautions were taken to avoid leaving any scent marks or disturbances that could potentially alter behavior patterns of other individuals in the group. This protocol was considered non-invasive according to Taberlet et al.'s definition, as it caused minimal disturbance to the animals' natural behaviors and habits.
--------------------------------------------------

--------------------------------------------------
"For the collection of genetic samples from wild animals, non-invasive methods were employed to minimize stress and disturbance. Fecal samples were collected as they are easily accessible and do not require direct contact with the animal. Hair snares baited with aromatic substances were used to collect shed hair from bears (Ursus arctos). The snares were checked daily and any captured hairs were removed and stored in individual zip-top bags labeled with the collection date and location. To minimize disturbance, the snares were placed along trails or near known bear activity areas but not within direct sight of the den or cubs. A total of five samples were collected from each individual over a three-month period to ensure sufficient DNA for genetic analysis while minimizing the impact on the animal's health."

--------------------------------------------------
For non-invasive genetic sampling of wild animals, we collected shed hair from mammals using barbed wire traps baited with peanut butter. Traps were set up along trails and at watering holes, where animal activity was observed. Hair samples were collected every two months over a period of one year to ensure sufficient genetic material for analysis while minimizing disturbance to the animals' natural behavior. To reduce stress and prevent injury, traps were checked daily, and hair samples were carefully removed using sterile forceps without touching the animal's body. All trapping procedures followed guidelines from the American Society of Mammalogists to minimize impact on wildlife populations.

This method avoids the necessity for animal capture or handling, thus minimizing stress and potential injury to the animals. The procedure involves placing a sterile artificial vagina onto the penis of the male during copulation with a female in estrus. The artificial vagina is designed to mimic the temperature, pH, and lubrication of the female reproductive tract, thereby stimulating ejaculation without causing discomfort or harm to the male. The collected samples were then stored at -80°C until DNA extraction could be performed. This method is considered non-invasive as it does not require any direct contact with the animal, reduces stress levels, and has no apparent adverse effects on fertility or overall health of the males involved in the study.
