For non-invasive genetic sampling, we collected fecal samples from wild giant pandas (Ailuropoda melanoleuca) using barbed wire traps baited with fresh bamboo leaves. These traps were designed to capture fur but not harm the animals. We placed the traps along trails and at known feeding sites within each of the four study areas. Fecal samples were collected every three days over a two-week period, ensuring minimal disturbance to the animals' natural behaviors while maximizing the chances of obtaining multiple samples per individual. To minimize stress and maximize sample recovery, we used sterile gloves when handling samples and stored them in liquid nitrogen within 24 hours of collection. Our sampling method was noninvasive according to Taberlet et al.'s (1997) definition, as it did not cause physical harm or long-term impact on the animals.

Document hypothétique 3:
--------------------------------------------------
In this study, we employed noninvasive genetic sampling methods to collect DNA from wild animal populations. The primary sample type obtained was feces, which were collected passively using baited traps. The trapping devices consisted of wire mesh cages containing a lure (e.g., food or scent) designed to attract the target species. Feces were allowed to accumulate inside the trap over several days before being collected using sterile forceps and stored in individualWhirl-Pak bags containing silica gel desiccant. This passive sampling approach minimized animal contact and disturbance, thus reducing stress and potential harm to the animals. Samples were collected at intervals of approximately two weeks to limit the chance of collecting multiple samples from the same individual. A total of 30-50 samples were collected per species, depending on the abundance and distribution of the target populations. This noninvasive approach allowed us to estimate population size and genetic diversity while minimizing invasiveness according to Taberlet et al.'s definition (Taberlet et al., 1997).
