Document hypothétique 3:
--------------------------------------------------
For genetic analysis of wild animals, non-invasive sampling methods are often preferred to minimize disturbance and stress. In this study, we collected fecal samples from African forest elephants (Loxodonta africana) as they provide a reliable source of DNA for genetic analyses. Fecal samples were collected along known elephant paths or at watering holes using sterile gloves and plastic bags. To minimize the risk of cross-contamination between samples, we wore new disposable gloves for each collection. The sampling was conducted during the early morning hours when elephants are most active, allowing us to collect fresh samples within a short time frame. We collected approximately 50 grams of feces from each sample, ensuring that at least one sample per elephant group was obtained every two weeks to maximize genetic coverage while minimizing disturbance to the animals. All samples were stored in liquid nitrogen within four hours of collection to preserve DNA integrity. Ethics approval was granted for this study, and all methods were carried out in accordance with relevant guidelines and regulations to minimize stress and perturbation to the elephants.
