Methods:

The sampling protocol employed in this study involved the collection of non-invasive DNA samples from wild wolf populations. The primary method used was hair snare traps baited with fresh beef and equipped with barbed wire loops designed to capture animal hairs without physically capturing the animals. These traps were set up along known wolf travel routes, with an average trap spacing of 2 km and a trapping duration of 7 days per site. A total of 150 traps were deployed across three different locations.

Upon retrieval, hair samples were collected using sterile forceps and placed into individual Whirl-Pak bags. To minimize the impact on the animals, traps were checked daily and removed once a sufficient quantity of hairs was obtained. Additionally, all trapping sites were monitored for signs of stress or disturbance to the wolves, with no visible negative effects observed.

This non-invasive sampling method allowed us to obtain high-quality DNA samples while minimizing animal handling and thus reducing potential stressors. However, it should be noted that this approach may have a lower success rate compared to invasive methods such as blood sampling, which could potentially limit sample size. Nonetheless, the ethical considerations and minimal impact on wildlife make this method preferable for studying wild wolf populations.
--------------------------------------------------

Document hypothétique 2:
--------------------------------------------------
"For non-invasive genetic sampling (NGS), we used hair snares baited with peanut butter and rolled oats. These passive devices allow for the collection of hair samples without direct contact or handling of animals. The snares were checked daily for five consecutive days, and any captured hairs were collected using sterile forceps and placed into individual zip-top bags. A total of 10-20 hairs was targeted per sample to ensure sufficient DNA yield for analysis. To minimize disturbance and stress to the animals, snares were deployed in areas with low visibility and removed immediately upon capture. Additionally, no bait was left behind after collection to avoid attracting other animals."
--------------------------------------------------

Document hypothétique 3:
--------------------------------------------------
For genetic analysis of wild animal populations, non-invasive sampling methods were employed to minimize disturbance and stress to the animals. Fecal samples were collected as they contain sufficient amounts of DNA for downstream analyses. The sampling protocol involved regular patrols along known animal trails and habitats using trained field assistants. Upon discovering fresh scat, a small portion was carefully collected using sterile forcps and placed into individual tubes containing lysis buffer to preserve the DNA. To prevent cross-contamination between samples, new gloves were worn for each collection event. This passive sampling method allowed us to obtain valuable genetic data without direct contact with the animals or causing perturbation to their habitats.
