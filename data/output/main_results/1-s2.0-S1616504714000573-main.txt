Methods:

DNA sampling protocols varied depending on the target species and its ecological characteristics. For non-invasive sampling, hair snares were used to collect DNA from fur-bearing animals such as bears and wolves. These consisted of barbed wire coated with a sterile, inert lure (e.g., vanilla extract) secured to trees or other structures in areas frequented by the target species. Hair samples were collected every two weeks during the active season to maximize yield while minimizing disturbance.

For-invasive sampling, tissue punches were taken from ear biopsies of ungulates like deer and elk. Animals were captured using Clover traps baited with corn and checked daily to minimize stress. Upon capture, individuals were sedated with a combination of carfentanil and medetomidine using a dart gun, ensuring proper dosage based on body weight. Ear tissue punches (3-5 mm) were taken from both ears using a sterile punch tool, taking care not to damage the ear cartilage. Following sampling, animals were administered atipamezole and naltrexone reversal agents before release.

In both cases, samples were stored in 95% ethanol and preserved at -20°C until further processing for DNA extraction. All procedures followed guidelines approved by relevant authorities (e.g., IACUC) to minimize stress, injury, and perturbation of sampled animals.
--------------------------------------------------

Document hypothétique 2:
--------------------------------------------------
During our genetic analysis of wild animal populations, we employed two primary sampling methods. The first involved collecting tissue samples from deceased animals found during routine monitoring activities in the study area. This passive sampling method minimized disturbance to live animals and was considered non-invasive according to Taberlet's definition. Tissue samples were collected using sterilized forceps and stored in individual cryovials containing RNAlater™ solution to preserve DNA integrity.

The second method involved capturing live individuals for blood sampling, which was deemed moderately invasive due to the temporary restraint of animals. We used Tomahawk live traps baited with peanut butter to capture small mammals such as rodents and shrews. Upon capture, animals were briefly anesthetized using isoflurane for safe handling and minimal stress. Approximately 50µL of blood was collected from the saphenous vein using a sterile lancet and microcentrifuge tubes coated with EDTA as an anticoagulant. Blood samples were then stored at -20°C until DNA extraction. Throughout the sampling process, we adhered to strict ethical guidelines to ensure animal welfare, including maintaining optimal temperatures during anesthesia and minimizing handling times.
