In this study, we employed a non-invasive sampling method using carnivore hair traps. These consisted of barbed wire loops baited with fur from a sympatric species and secured to trees along known animal trails. The traps were checked weekly for 3 months, and any captured animals were immediately released after hair samples were collected using sterile forceps. To minimize disturbance, no more than five hairs were taken from each individual. All trapping locations were marked with GPS coordinates and recorded in a logbook. This method allowed us to obtain genetic material without causing significant stress or harm to the wildlife populations under study.

Document hypothétique 2:
--------------------------------------------------
In this study, we employed a non-invasive sampling method using carrion flies (Diptera: Calliphoridae) to collect DNA from wildlife. This approach minimizes disturbance and stress to the animals, aligning with Taberlet's definition of non-invasiveness. Flies were attracted to baited traps containing decaying meat, allowing us to capture them without direct contact with the target species. We used modified Kennedy traps equipped with preservative solution to maintain sample integrity. Fly collections occurred at weekly intervals, with each trap yielding approximately 50 individuals per sampling event. Samples were stored at -20°C until DNA extraction using a standard salt extraction protocol. This method enabled us to collect sufficient genetic material from wild animals without requiring capture or handling, thus reducing potential harm and maintaining ethical considerations.
--------------------------------------------------

Document hypothétique 3:
--------------------------------------------------
"For this study, we employed a non-invasive DNA sampling method using carrion flies (Chrysomya megacephala) as collectors of animal tissue samples. Adult flies were collected from wild populations and individually housed in cages with sugar water and decaying meat as food sources. When necessary for sampling, the flies were transferred to clean cages equipped with sterile gauze pads moistened with distilled water to encourage regurgitation of ingested tissue samples. The flies were allowed to remain in these cages for 24 hours before being frozen at -80°C until further processing. This method minimizes disturbance to the animals while providing adequate DNA sample size for genetic analysis, thus aligning with Taberlet's definition of low-invasive sampling."
