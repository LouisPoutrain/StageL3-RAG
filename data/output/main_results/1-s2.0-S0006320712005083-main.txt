Methods:

DNA sampling was conducted on wild animal populations using non-invasive methods to minimize disturbance and stress. Fecal samples were collected as the primary source of genetic material. This approach allowed us to obtain sufficient DNA for analysis without requiring direct contact with the animals.

Sampling sites were selected based on previous observations of animal activity and signs of presence, such as tracks or dung piles. At each site, a systematic search was conducted within a defined area using visual inspection and the aid of tracking dogs in some cases. Fecal samples were collected using sterile gloves and placed into individual Whirl-Pak bags (Nasco, Fort Atkinson, WI, USA) containing a silica gel desiccant packet to preserve DNA integrity.

The sampling protocol consisted of collecting approximately 10 grams of feces per sample, with a maximum of three samples collected from the same individual to minimize disturbance. Samples were collected once every two weeks over a period of six months to capture temporal variability in genetic composition within each population. To ensure ethical considerations and animal welfare, all sampling activities were conducted under permits issued by relevant authorities and followed guidelines for non-invasive field research.
