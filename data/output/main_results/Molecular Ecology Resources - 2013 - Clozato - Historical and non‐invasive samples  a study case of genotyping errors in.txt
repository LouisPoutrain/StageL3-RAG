--------------------------------------------------
In this study, we employed non-invasive sampling methods to collect DNA from wild animal populations. We focused on feces samples due to their abundant availability and the minimal invasiveness of their collection. Feces were collected using sterile gloves and plastic bags at regular intervals along established trails or near known animal dens. Samples were stored in individual Whirl-Pak bags containing silica beads to preserve DNA integrity during transportation back to the laboratory. To minimize disturbance to animals, we avoided collecting samples from areas with visible signs of recent animal activity and ensured that our presence did not cause excessive stress or alteration of natural behaviors. Our sampling protocol was designed to collect a maximum of five feces per individual over a period of one week, with at least 10 meters between each collection site to minimize the risk of cross-contamination. This approach allowed us to maximize the amount of genetic material obtained while minimizing the impact on animal welfare according to Taberlet et al.'s definition of non-invasive sampling.

Document hypothétique 3:
--------------------------------------------------
"To obtain non-invasive genetic samples from wild animals, we employed hair snare traps baited with bacon. These traps were designed to capture hairs without direct contact or manipulation of the animal. The traps were checked daily for three consecutive days each month over a period of six months. Once captured, hairs were stored in individual sterile bags containing silica gel desiccant to preserve DNA quality. A total of 20-30 hairs per sample were collected from each trap, ensuring that at least one hair was plucked from the root to maximize the amount of usable DNA. To minimize animal stress and perturbation, traps were set up in areas with low visibility and noise levels. Additionally, bait was replenished frequently to attract animals and maintain their interest despite potential disturbances caused by researchers."
