In this study, we employed non-invasive genetic sampling methods to collect DNA from the scats of the target species. We used camera traps baited with eucalyptus leaves and infrared illumination to locate active den sites. Once located, scats were collected using sterile forceps and placed in individual sterile bags, which were then stored at -20°C until further processing. To minimize disturbance, we limited our sampling frequency to once every two weeks per den site, ensuring that each animal had sufficient time to produce new scats between samples. Additionally, we took precautions to avoid capturing or manipulating the animals directly, adhering to ethical guidelines for non-invasive wildlife research.
