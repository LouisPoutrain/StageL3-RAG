DNA sampling protocols for wildlife genetic analysis often involve non-invasive methods to minimize disturbance and stress to the animals. In this study, we employed a passive collection technique using hair snares baited with scent attractants. The hair snares consisted of barbed wire loops attached to trees or other stable structures along animal trails. The hair samples were stored in individual zip-top bags and placed on dry ice immediately after collection to preserve DNA integrity. Ethical considerations included minimizing the impact on animal behavior by placing snares at least 50 meters apart and checking them regularly to avoid prolonged capture. In this study, we employed non-invasive DNA sampling methods to minimize disturbance to wild animal populations. We focused on collecting fecal samples due to their abundance and ease of collection in the African rain forest habitat. To do so, we established transects within the study area and systematically searched for fresh feces along these paths using visual cues such as shape, moisture content, and associated signs of recent activity. To further reduce impact on wildlife, we limited our sampling frequency to once every two weeks at each site and avoided collecting from areas with evident signs of animal stress or perturbation.
