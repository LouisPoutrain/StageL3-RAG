Methods:

Non-invasive DNA sampling was employed to minimize disturbance and stress on the targeted wild animal populations. Fecal samples were collected as the primary source of genetic material. This method allowed for a passive collection approach, ensuring that animals did not need to be captured or handled.

Feces were collected from predefined sites within the study areas using sterile gloves and placed into individual Whirl-Pak bags (Nasco, USA). Sampling was conducted monthly over a period of one year to ensure sufficient temporal coverage and to monitor population fluctuations. Approximately 5-10 grams of feces were collected per sample, with care taken not to disturb the immediate environment to avoid contamination.

To minimize potential biases associated with individual variation in defecation rates or site fidelity, multiple sampling sites were established within each study area. These sites were chosen based on signs of animal activity such as tracks, trails, and feeding remains. Additionally, precautions were taken to avoid collecting samples from the same individual repeatedly by using genetic identification methods (i.e., microsatellite analysis) to track unique genotypes.

Ethical considerations included maintaining a safe distance from animals while sampling and ensuring that no animals were disturbed during collection procedures. All protocols were approved by relevant institutional animal care committees, adhering to guidelines for the treatment of wild animals in scientific research.
