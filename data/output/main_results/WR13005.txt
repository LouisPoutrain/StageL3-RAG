Extraction of DNA from wildlife samples is crucial for genetic analysis. In this study, we collected non-invasive tissue samples from free-ranging wolves (Canis lupus) using remote hair-snaring techniques to minimize animal contact and disturbance. Live-capture traps baited with deer carcasses were used, along with barbed wire nooses attached to trees. Once a wolf was caught in the snare, it was immobilized with an intramuscular injection of ketamine and xylazine. After obtaining tissue samples from the ear or toe using sterile forceps and scalpel blades, animals were revived using yohimbine and released at the capture site. Samples were stored in 95% ethanol at room temperature until DNA extraction. This protocol was approved by local ethical committees and followed guidelines to minimize stress and injury to the animals, as per Taberlet's definition of non-invasive sampling methods.
