Methods:
To collect DNA samples from wild animals for genetic analysis, we employed non-invasive sampling methods to minimize disturbance and stress on the target populations. We focused on two main types of samples: fecal and hair.

For fecal sample collection, we used a protocol adapted from previous studies (e.g., Pardo et al., 2013). Camera traps were deployed along trails and near known water sources in the study areas to monitor animal activity. Once fresh scats were identified, they were carefully collected using sterile gloves and placed in individual Whirl-Pak bags. We ensured that each sample was at least 5 meters away from other samples to avoid cross-contamination. This protocol allowed us to collect approximately 20-30 samples per month, depending on animal abundance.

For hair sampling, we used barbed wire loops secured to trees along trails and near water sources. These loops were checked every two weeks for trapped hairs. Once hairs were collected, they were carefully removed from the loops using sterile forceps and placed in individual paper envelopes. This method yielded around 10-20 samples per month, with minimal disturbance to the animals.

In both cases, we took precautions to minimize stress and disturbance to the animals. For fecal sampling, we ensured that no animal was approached directly while collecting scats. For hair sampling, barbed wire loops were checked during periods of low animal activity to avoid direct contact with the animals. All samples were stored at room temperature until DNA extraction.

These non-invasive methods allowed us to collect sufficient genetic material for analysis without significantly impacting the target populations, in accordance with Taberlet's definition of invasive sampling (Taberlet et al., 2018).

A total of 10 samples were collected per species, with a maximum of one sample per individual per day to avoid over-sampling and reduce potential stress on individuals. Samples were stored in liquid nitrogen within two hours of collection to preserve DNA integrity."
