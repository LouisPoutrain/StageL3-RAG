=== Documents hypothétiques générés ===

Document hypothétique 1:
--------------------------------------------------
The genetic material used for analysis was extracted from non-invasively collected fecal samples of the study population. Feces were collected along animal trails and at communal defecation sites using sterile gloves and disposable plastic bags. The collection process involved visiting these sites on foot every other day, between dawn and noon, to minimize disturbance to the animals. A total of 100 samples were collected over a period of six months. To preserve DNA integrity, each sample was stored in individual Whirl-Pak bags containing silica beads and transported to the laboratory within 48 hours of collection where they were frozen at -20°C until further processing. This noninvasive approach allowed us to estimate population size and genetic diversity without causing stress or perturbation to the animal population, thus adhering to ethical considerations for wildlife research as defined by Taberlet et al. (2018).
--------------------------------------------------

Document hypothétique 2:
--------------------------------------------------
Methods:

Non-invasive sampling was employed to collect DNA from the remaining F individuals. Feces were collected as the primary sample type due to their accessibility and abundance in the study area. To minimize disturbance to animals, we used indirect, passive sampling methods involving hair snares baited with food attractants specific to each species (i.e., peanut butter for bears and chicken for wolves). Hair snares consisted of barbed wire loops attached to trees or other stable structures along known animal trails.

The snares were checked daily for new samples, and once detected, they were collected using sterile forceps and placed in individual zip-top bags labeled with the date and location. To ensure sufficient DNA yield, a minimum of three hairs was required per sample. Additionally, to reduce the risk of cross-contamination between samples, each hair snare was cleaned with 70% ethanol before reuse.

To minimize potential stress and disturbance to animals, hair snares were placed at least 5 meters from trails or other frequented areas. Furthermore, all sampling activities were conducted during daylight hours to avoid disturbing nocturnal animals during their active periods.
--------------------------------------------------

Document hypothétique 3:
--------------------------------------------------
In this study, we employed non-invasive genetic sampling methods to collect DNA from the remaining F. We targeted fecal samples as they are abundant and informative for population genetic studies (Waits & Paetkau, 2005). We used barbed wire traps baited with rotting meat to capture individuals without direct contact. Once captured, animals were allowed to defecate naturally in the trap before release. Fecal samples were collected using sterile gloves and forceps, ensuring minimal disturbance to the animal or its environment. Samples were stored in individual Whirl-Pak bags containing silica gel desiccant at room temperature until extraction. This protocol was designed to minimize stress and injury to the animals while maximizing the quantity and quality of DNA obtained from non-invasively collected samples (Taberlet et al., 2019).
