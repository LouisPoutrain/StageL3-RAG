In order to obtain genetic samples from wild animals for genetic analysis, we employed non-invasive sampling methods to minimize disturbance and stress to the animals. We collected fecal samples from wolves (Canis lupus) using barricaded trails lined with plastic sheets that were replaced daily. The plastic sheets were secured at both ends using stakes and were checked regularly for fresh scat. Once discovered, the scat was carefully collected using sterile gloves, placed in individual zip-top bags, labeled with a unique identifier code, and stored on ice before being transported to the laboratory for DNA extraction. This method allowed us to obtain sufficient DNA for genetic analysis while minimizing the impact on the wolves' natural behavior and habitat. Additionally, this protocol is considered non-invasive according to Taberlet's definition as it does not involve physical contact with the animals or cause any harm or perturbation to their well-being.

Document hypothétique 3:
--------------------------------------------------
In this study, we employed non-invasive genetic sampling methods to minimize disturbance to the wild animal populations. We focused on collecting fecal samples, as they provide a reliable source of DNA and allow for repeated sampling from the same individual over time. Fecal collections were performed using a combination of visual observations and opportunistic searches along established trails and near known dens or resting sites. Once located, fecal samples were collected using sterile forcps and placed into individual zip-top plastic bags. Each bag was labeled with a unique sample ID number and stored on ice in a cooler until transportation to the laboratory for DNA extraction and analysis. To maximize the chances of obtaining high-quality DNA, we targeted fresh fecal samples (<72 hours old) and collected multiple samples (n=3-5) from each individual whenever possible. Prior to sampling, we ensured that no animals were present or within sight of the collection area to minimize disturbance. Additionally, all field protocols were approved by the appropriate animal care and use committee to ensure compliance with ethical guidelines for wildlife research.
