The sampling method employed in this study involved the collection of non-invasive genetic samples from wild animal populations. Fecal samples were chosen as the primary source due to their accessibility and informative value for genetic analysis. The animals were not contacted or manipulated during the sampling process, minimizing potential stress and disturbance.

Fecal collection was performed using barbless pitfall traps baited with a suitable attractant specific to each target species. Traps were set up along trails and near water sources where animal activity was observed. Sampling sites were marked with GPS coordinates to ensure accurate relocation during subsequent sampling events.

Samples were collected on a weekly basis over a period of six months, with approximately 10-20 grams of fecal matter being retrieved from each trap per visit. This frequency and quantity ensured adequate representation of the target populations while preventing over-exploitation of individual animals. Collected samples were immediately stored in cryovials containing RNAlater™ to preserve DNA integrity until further processing.

Throughout the sampling process, precautions were taken to minimize disturbances to animal behavior and habitats. Traps were checked daily to reduce capture duration, and bait was replaced as needed to maintain attractiveness. Additionally, all traps were visually inspected for signs of distress or injury before deployment. If any adverse effects were observed, trapping efforts were immediately ceased in that location.

In accordance with Taberlet et al.'s definition of invasivity, this non-invasive approach can be considered low-impact as it minimizes direct contact with animals and reduces potential stress and disturbance associated with capture and manipulation methods.
