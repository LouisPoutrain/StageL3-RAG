In this study, we employed non-invasive DNA sampling methods to minimize disturbance to wild animal populations. Fecal samples were collected as the primary source of DNA, as they are rich in genetic material and can be obtained without direct contact with animals. We used a combination of opportunistic collection during field observations and systematic searches along known animal trails and near feeding sites using sterile gloves and tweezers for handling. Sample collection was conducted daily during fieldwork periods to maximize the chances of obtaining fresh samples. A total of 300 fecal samples were collected over three months, with an average of ten samples per week. To preserve DNA integrity, samples were stored in individual zip-top bags containing a silica desiccant packet and frozen at -20°C within 12 hours of collection. Ethical considerations included minimizing disturbance to animals' habitats and avoiding collecting during sensitive periods such as mating or breeding seasons. Additionally, we ensured that no signs of stress or injury were observed following sampling activities.
--------------------------------------------------

In this study, we employed non-invasive genetic sampling methods to minimize disturbance to wild animal populations. Tissue samples were collected from feces using a sterile cotton swab inserted into the fecal matter and rotated gently to maximize DNA yield (waiting for at least 24 hours between collections). A total of 50 samples were collected over six months, with a frequency of approximately one sample per week per individual. To ensure ethical considerations, all sampling was conducted during nighttime hours when animals were less active, reducing the risk of disturbing their natural behaviors or causing stress. Additionally, care was taken to avoid collecting from fresh fecal material to minimize contamination and maintain animal health.
