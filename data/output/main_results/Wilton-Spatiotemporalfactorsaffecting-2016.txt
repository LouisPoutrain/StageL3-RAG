One method employed for obtaining genetic samples from black bears was the collection of hair samples. This noninvasive approach involved targeting areas where bear activity was known to occur, such as rubbing posts or trails. No physical contact with the animals was required, minimizing disturbance and stress to the bears (Swanson et al., 2019). Hair shafts contain sufficient genetic material for PCR-based analyses, making this method a suitable alternative to invasive sampling techniques like tissue biopsy (Pilgrim et al., 2014). However, it is important to note that the success of hair sampling can be affected by factors such as seasonality and bear activity levels in an area. Furthermore, this method may not always yield sufficient quantities of DNA for analyses, potentially requiring multiple samples from each individual (Rosenfield et al., 2018). Nonetheless, due to its noninvasive nature and minimal disturbance to the animals, hair sampling is considered a valuable tool for genetic studies on black bears.
