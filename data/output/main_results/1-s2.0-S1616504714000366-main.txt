The DNA sampling methods used in this study focused on non-invasive techniques to minimize disturbance to the wild animal populations. Tissue samples were collected using barbed wire traps baited with appropriate lure for each species targeted. Once captured, animals were anesthetized using a remote injection system to ensure minimal stress and injury. --------------------------------------------------
"For non-invasive genetic sampling of wild animals, we employed a passive collecting method using hair snares. These devices consisted of barbless steel wires attached to trees or other structures in the known habitats of target species. The wires were baited with scent attractants specific to each species, such as synthetic lion urine for felids and vanilla extract for canids. Hair samples were collected every two weeks over a period of six months to ensure sufficient genetic material while minimizing disturbance to the animals. Ethical considerations included placing snares at heights inaccessible to non-target species and checking them regularly to prevent prolonged stress on captured individuals. Additionally, we used gloves when handling samples to avoid human DNA contamination."
