Document hypothétique 1:
--------------------------------------------------
During fieldwork in the Kahuzi-Biega National Park, non-invasive genetic sampling methods were employed to collect DNA from wild chimpanzees (Pan troglodytes schwenfurthii). Fecal samples were collected as they contained sufficient DNA for genetic analyses and did not require direct contact with the animals. The researchers used visual cues such as freshness of feces and absence of insect activity to determine sample collection time. Feces were collected using sterile gloves, placed in individual plastic bags, sealed, labeled, and stored in liquid nitrogen within four hours of collection to preserve DNA integrity.

Sampling was conducted along established trails and at feeding sites where chimpanzee presence had been confirmed by previous observations. To minimize disturbance to the animals, sampling was performed during the early morning or late afternoon when chimpanzees were less active. No direct observation of chimpanzees was permitted during sampling to reduce stress and perturbation.

The genetic sampling protocol involved collecting feces at each site once a week for four consecutive weeks, with no more than one sample collected per day from any given location. This frequency allowed for adequate representation of the chimpanzee community while minimizing the potential impact on individual animals' health due to excessive sample collection. A total of 170 fecal samples were collected during the study period.

To ensure ethical considerations and minimize stress, researchers maintained a distance of at least 25 meters from any visible chimpanzees or signs of recent activity. Furthermore, no food was provided as bait to attract chimpanzees, and care was taken not to disturb the animals' natural behavior.
--------------------------------------------------

Document hypothétique 2:
--------------------------------------------------
For genetic analysis of wild animals, non-invasive sampling methods are often preferable to minimize disturbance and stress on the target species. In our study of chimpanzees in Kahuzi-Biega, we collected fecal samples as they provide sufficient DNA for genetic analyses while being non-invasively obtained.

We used indirect methods to collect feces from known chimpanzee nests within their territories. We systematically searched for fresh samples around the nest periphery and marked the location using a Global Positioning System (GPS). Fecal samples were collected using sterile gloves, placed in plastic bags, and stored on ice before being transferred to a -20°C freezer until DNA extraction.

Our sampling protocol involved collecting one fecal sample per individual whenever possible, with a maximum of three samples per week from the same nest to minimize disturbance. This frequency allowed us to estimate community size and genetic diversity while minimizing potential impacts on chimpanzee behavior and habitat use. Ethical considerations included maintaining a safe distance from chimpanzees during collection to avoid stress or perturbation, and ensuring that no personal protective equipment was left behind at the sampling site.
--------------------------------------------------
