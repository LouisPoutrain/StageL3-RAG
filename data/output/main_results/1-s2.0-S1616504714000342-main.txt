"DNA sampling methods used in wild animal genetic analysis"

In this study, we employed non-invasive DNA sampling methods to minimize disturbance and stress to the targeted wildlife populations. Our primary sampling technique involved the collection of fecal samples using barbed wire loops placed along animal trails or beneath feeding platforms. We utilized latex gloves to handle the samples and prevent cross-contamination, storing them in sterile 50 mL centrifuge tubes containing silica beads for later extraction of DNA.

We established a standardized protocol with weekly collections over a period of four months. Each sample was geo-referenced using a GPS device, and care was taken to ensure that no more than five samples were collected from any single individual during the study period to avoid excessive disturbance. We also monitored animal behavior before, during, and after sampling to assess any signs of stress or perturbation. No significant changes in behavior were observed, indicating that our non-invasive approach had minimal impact on the animals.

Additionally, we employed hair snare traps baited with peanut butter to collect shed hairs for genetic analysis. These traps were checked daily and cleared promptly to minimize animal distress. Hair samples were stored dry in paper envelopes until DNA extraction could be performed.
--------------------------------------------------

Document hypothétique 2:
--------------------------------------------------
The sampling method used for this study was non-invasive, focusing on the collection of fecal samples from wild animals. Feces were collected using sterile gloves and placed in individual Whirl-Pak bags (Nasco, Fort Atkinson, WI, USA) to minimize contamination and preserve DNA integrity. Sampling was conducted along predetermined transects within each site, with a minimum distance of 50 meters between consecutive samples to avoid pseudoreplication. Each sample was geo-referenced using a GPS device (Garmin eTrex H, Olathe, KS, USA) and collected approximately every 10 days over a period of three months to ensure sufficient genetic material while minimizing disturbance to the animals' habitats. Ethically, caution was taken to avoid areas with visible animal activity or signs of stress during sampling to minimize perturbation of wildlife.
--------------------------------------------------

Document hypothétique 3:
--------------------------------------------------
Methods:

Non-invasive fecal sampling was employed to collect genetic material from wild mountain gorillas (Gorilla gorilla beringei) in Volcanoes National Park, Rwanda. Fresh fecal samples were collected opportunistically during routine patrols by park rangers and research teams. Each sample was located using GPS coordinates and marked with a unique identifier for tracking purposes. Samples were stored individually in sterile zip-top bags and kept cool until transportation to the laboratory where they were stored at -20°C pending DNA extraction.

To minimize disturbance to the gorillas, care was taken not to approach their nests or trails too closely during sample collection. Additionally, samples were collected only from areas where there was no evidence of recent gorilla activity to further reduce the risk of disturbing the animals.
