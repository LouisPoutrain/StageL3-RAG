In this study, non-invasive DNA sampling methods were employed to minimize disturbance to the endangered Cantabrian brown bear population. Fecal samples were collected as they provide a reliable source of genetic material and do not require direct contact with the animal. The collection process involved surveying bear habitats on foot or using vehicles, searching for fresh scat, and marking their locations using GPS. Additionally, all samples were collected under permits issued by local authorities, ensuring compliance with ethical guidelines for wildlife research.

In this study, non-invasive DNA sampling methods were employed to minimize disturbance and stress to the endangered Cantabrian brown bear population. Fecal samples were collected as they provide a reliable source of DNA for genetic analysis while allowing for minimal animal contact. Sampling was conducted along known bear activity routes using visual surveys to locate fresh scat. Sampling occurred monthly during the bears' active season (April to October) with a target of collecting five samples per animal annually. To ensure ethical considerations, sampling teams maintained a distance from bears and refrained from handling animals directly, thus minimizing potential disturbance or stress. Additionally, no baits were used to attract bears, further reducing the risk of altering natural behaviors or causing harm.
