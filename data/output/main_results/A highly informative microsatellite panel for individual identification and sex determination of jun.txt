In this study, we employed non-invasive genetic sampling techniques to collect DNA from wild Juniper trees (Juniperus virginiana). The primary method used was the collection of needles shed naturally by the trees. Needles were collected from the forest floor around the base of each targeted tree using sterile forceps and placed in individual labeled envelopes. A total of 20 needles per tree were collected, ensuring that at least five needles were collected from different parts of the crown to maximize genetic diversity within each sample. Collections were conducted monthly for a period of one year to capture seasonal variations in needle shedding patterns. To minimize disturbance to the trees, collections were made during the early morning hours when environmental conditions were calm and animal activity was low. Additionally, care was taken not to collect more than 1% of the total needle biomass from any given tree to prevent potential stress or negative impacts on plant health.
