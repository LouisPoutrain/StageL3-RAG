Extraction of genetic material from wild animal populations often involves non-invasive sampling methods to minimize disturbance and stress. In this study, we collected fecal samples from polar bears (Ursus maritimus) in the Arctic using remote hair snares. These snares consisted of monofilament fishing line attached to a board with a piece of polar bear hair glued onto it as bait. The snares were placed along known polar bear travel routes and checked daily for captures. Upon capture, a small amount of fur was removed from the trapped animal using sterile scissors, and the snare was immediately released. Fecal samples were collected from the surroundings of the captured site within a 50-meter radius over a period of three days. A total of 100 fecal samples were collected over six months, with an average of approximately 16.7 samples per month. All samples were stored in sterile Whirl-Pak bags at -20°C until further processing for DNA extraction using established protocols. This non-invasive sampling method allowed us to minimize disturbance to the polar bear populations while obtaining sufficient genetic material for our analysis.
--------------------------------------------------

Document hypothétique 2:
--------------------------------------------------
Methods:

To collect DNA samples from free-ranging polar bears (Ursus maritimus), we employed non-invasive hair snare traps. These traps consisted of monofilament fishing line attached to trees or stakes in areas where polar bears were known to frequent, such as along coastlines and near seal haul-out sites. The lines were baited with polar bear attractants, such as fatty acids and seal scent, to increase the likelihood of capture.

Upon activation, the traps would snare hairs from passing polar bears without requiring physical contact or manipulation of the animals. Hair samples were collected on a monthly basis throughout the sampling period to maximize genetic diversity within our dataset. Each hair sample was stored individually in a sealed plastic bag containing silica gel desiccant to preserve DNA integrity until laboratory analysis.

To minimize potential disturbance and stress to the polar bears, we ensured that traps were checked daily and that any captured hairs were promptly removed. Additionally, trap locations were rotated regularly to avoid overuse of specific areas and potential impacts on bear behavior or habitat use.
--------------------------------------------------

Once found, hairs were carefully removed using clean forceps and stored in individual nylon mesh bags labeled with collection date, location, and trap ID. This method minimizes stress and disturbance to animals, adhering to Taberlet et al.'s definition of non-invasive sampling."
