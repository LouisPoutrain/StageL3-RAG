Document hypothétique 1:
--------------------------------------------------
"Blood samples were collected from free-ranging jaguarundis (Herpailurus yagouaroundi) in Mexico using remote tranquillization. Animals were immobilized by dart gun injection of tiletamine/zolazepam and zoletil. Once sedated, blood was extracted from the femoral vein using sterile needles and syringes. Samples were stored in lithium heparin tubes at room temperature until centrifugation to separate plasma, which was then frozen at -20°C. All procedures were conducted following approved protocols by the Mexican authorities (permit SGPA/DGVS/03645) ensuring minimal stress and disturbance to the animals."
--------------------------------------------------

Document hypothétique 2:
--------------------------------------------------
In this study, we employed non-invasive genetic sampling methods to collect DNA from jaguars (Panthera onca) in Mexico. The primary samples collected were feces, which were systematically searched for and located along trails and other known jaguar habitats using GPS tracking and visual inspection. Upon discovery, fecal samples were carefully collected using sterile gloves and placed into individual Whirl-Pak bags containing a solution of 20% dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) and EDTA to preserve the DNA. Sampling was conducted monthly over a period of two years to ensure sufficient genetic material for population analysis while minimizing disturbance to the animals. Ethical considerations included maintaining a respectful distance from the animals, avoiding collection during sensitive periods such as mating or cub rearing, and ensuring that sampling did not negatively impact the animals' habitats. This non-invasive approach is considered minimally invasive according to Taberlet's definition, as it does not cause direct harm or significant disturbance to the targeted wildlife population.
--------------------------------------------------

Document hypothétique 3:
--------------------------------------------------
Methods:

Non-invasive DNA sampling was employed to minimize disturbance to jaguarundis. Fecal samples were collected along known trails and feeding sites within the study area. Gloves were worn during sample collection to prevent contamination with human DNA. Samples were stored in sterile tubes containing 95% ethanol at room temperature until extraction.

Sampling was conducted monthly between February 2010 and January 2012. A total of 387 samples were collected, with an average of 16 samples per month. To maximize the chances of obtaining high-quality DNA, only fresh feces (determined by color, texture, and the absence of insect activity) were collected.

To minimize disturbance to the animals, no bait or traps were used during sampling. Instead, researchers followed established trails and searched for fecal samples in areas where jaguarundis are known to frequent. This approach ensures that animals were not contacted directly and reduces potential stress caused by capture or manipulation.
