During genetic analysis of wild animals, non-invasive sampling methods are often preferred to minimize disturbance and stress on the target species. One such method involves collecting fecal samples from bears (Ursidae). Fecal sampling is achieved by monitoring known bear habitats and collecting fresh scats using sterile gloves and plastic bags. Samples are then stored in liquid nitrogen or dry ice until further processing.

The frequency of sampling varies depending on the specific research question, but generally, one sample per animal per season is sufficient for genetic analysis. In cases where individual identification is necessary, multiple samples from the same animal should be collected to ensure accurate genotyping.

To minimize disturbance and maintain ethical considerations, several precautions are taken during fecal collection. First, all field personnel receive proper training on bear behavior and safety protocols to avoid close contact with the animals. Additionally, sampling occurs only when bears are not present in the area, and all scats are collected within a short time frame to reduce animal detection.

Furthermore, this method is considered non-invasive according to Taberlet et al.'s definition, as it does not cause any direct physical harm or perturbation to the bears. However, care must be taken to avoid contamination between samples, which could introduce biases in genetic analysis.
--------------------------------------------------

"The DNA sampling methods employed in this study for wild animal genetic analysis consisted of non-invasive protocols. Fecal samples were collected as the primary type of biological material. This approach was chosen to minimize disturbance and stress to the animals, as per Taberlet's definition of low-invasive sampling methods. Capture and handling of the animals were not required, ensuring a reduced impact on their wellbeing. Custom-built hair traps baited with appropriate food items were used to collect samples over a period of seven days at each site. The traps consisted of a platform with a non-toxic adhesive layer coated with talcum powder to facilitate hair collection. Fecal samples were collected from the trap floor daily, stored in sterile tubes containing a preservation solution, and transported to the laboratory for further analysis."
