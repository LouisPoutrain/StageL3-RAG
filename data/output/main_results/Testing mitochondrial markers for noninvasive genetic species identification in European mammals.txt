**Sampling Methods:**

The study employed non-invasive genetic sampling methods to minimize disturbance to the targeted European mammal species. Feces were collected as the primary sample type due to their availability and informative content for DNA analysis. Two sampling protocols were implemented:

1. **Passive collection**: This method involved placing baited traps (containing food specific to each target species) at known activity sites. Traps were checked daily, and upon finding fresh feces, they were collected using sterile gloves or tweezers and placed in individual Whirl-Pak bags. This protocol ensured minimal disturbance to animals and was considered noninvasive according to Taberlet's definition.

2. **Direct collection**: In instances where direct observation of animal activity was possible (e.g., at feeding stations), fresh feces were collected directly from the ground using sterile gloves or tweezers and placed in Whirl-Pak bags. This method allowed for targeted sampling but required closer proximity to animals, potentially increasing disturbance, although still considered noninvasive following Taberlet's criteria.

In both protocols, approximately 10-20 grams of fecal material were collected per sample to ensure sufficient DNA yield for genetic analysis. Samples were stored at -20°C within six hours of collection to preserve DNA integrity. To minimize stress and perturbation to animals, precautions included avoiding sampling during sensitive periods (e.g., breeding season), limiting the number of samples taken from a single individual, and maintaining appropriate bait and water availability at trapping sites.
