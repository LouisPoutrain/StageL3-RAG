--------------------------------------------------
In this study, fecal samples were collected from coyotes (Canis latrans) as a non-invasive method to estimate abundance. Sampling was conducted using the remote hair snare technique with barbed wire snares baited with beaver carcasses. The snares were checked daily and removed upon capture or after 72 hours if no capture occurred. Upon capture, animals were briefly restrained for identification and sample collection. Fecal samples were collected from the ground around the capture site using sterile gloves and placed in cryovials containing a preservative solution. A total of five fecal samples were collected per animal over a period of three weeks to ensure sufficient DNA yield for genetic analysis. To minimize stress and disturbance, captures were performed during periods of low activity and animals were released as soon as possible after sample collection. This protocol was designed to minimize invasiveness according to Taberlet's definition, focusing on non-destructive sampling methods that cause minimal disruption to the animal's natural behavior and habitat.
--------------------------------------------------

Document hypothétique 3:
--------------------------------------------------
Fecal samples were collected from coyotes using non-invasive genetic sampling methods. The protocol involved placing barbed wire traps baited with chicken or beef liver in areas where coyote presence was suspected. Each trap was checked daily for three consecutive days, and any captured coyotes were immediately released after collecting fecal samples. A total of 10-15 fresh fecal samples were collected from each site, ensuring that no more than five samples were taken from a single individual to minimize the impact on the animal's health. Samples were stored in cryovials at -20°C until DNA extraction. This method allows for minimal disturbance to the animals while providing sufficient genetic material for analysis, making it an effective and non-invasive approach according to Taberlet et al.'s definition (Taberlet et al., 1997).
