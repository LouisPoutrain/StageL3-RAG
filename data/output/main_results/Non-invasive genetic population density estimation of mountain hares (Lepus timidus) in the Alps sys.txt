"To obtain genetic samples from mountain hares (Lepus timidus), we employed non-invasive methods to minimize disturbance and potential stress to the animals. Fecal samples were collected as they are easily accessible and contain sufficient DNA for genetic analyses. We used a protocol involving systematic searches along established transects in each of the three study areas, with a frequency of one sample collection per month over an 18-month period. During each search, we visually identified hare latrine sites based on characteristic signs such as pellet groups and urine scent marks. Once located, fecal samples were carefully collected using sterile tweezers and placed into individual Whirl-Pak bags (Nasco, Fort Atkinson, WI), which were then stored at -20°C until DNA extraction. This non-invasive approach allowed us to estimate population densities without directly handling or disturbing the animals."

This protocol involves searching for and collecting fresh fecal pellets within a predefined area or along transects, without any direct contact with the animals themselves. The primary advantage of this method is its non-invasive nature, as it does not require handling or capturing the animals, thus minimizing stress and potential harm to them. Additionally, the use of genetic markers from feces allows for the identification of individuals within a population without requiring invasive procedures such as tissue sampling or tagging.

However, while this method is generally considered non-invasive due to its minimal physical impact on the animals, it may still be considered minimally invasive in certain contexts. For instance, if the search for fecal pellets requires extensive disturbance of the habitat or if it occurs during critical periods such as mating season or when animals are caring for offspring, it could potentially induce stress responses or disrupt behaviors crucial for survival and reproduction. Therefore, careful consideration must be given to the timing and conduct of sampling activities to minimize any negative impacts on the studied populations.
