Methods:

DNA sampling was conducted non-invasively using hair snares and barbed wire fences. These devices allowed for the collection of shed hair samples from the target species without direct contact or manipulation of the animals. The hair snares consisted of monofilament fishing line attached to a tree or other stable structure, while the barbed wire fences were installed along known wildlife corridors.

Hair samples were collected monthly over a period of six months to ensure sufficient genetic material for analysis. Approximately 10-20 hairs were collected from each individual, with care taken not to disturb the surrounding environment and minimize potential stress to the animals. Samples were stored in coin envelopes labeled with unique identifiers and preserved in 95% ethanol until processing.

To further minimize animal disturbance, no bait or lure was used during sampling. Additionally, all equipment was checked regularly to ensure proper functioning and reduce the risk of harm to wildlife.

This non-invasive approach aligns with Taberlet et al.'s (2018) definition of low-impact sampling methods, as it causes minimal stress and disturbance to the animals while providing valuable genetic data.
